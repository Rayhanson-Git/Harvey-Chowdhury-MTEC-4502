## 2. Critical Reflection

AI helped me complete Assignment 1 more efficiently, but it did not choose my career for me. I was already interested in automation, AI systems, creative technology, and game development. Donna helped organize those interests, compare them against actual opportunities, and turn scattered information into work I could review. It saved time, especially on research, formatting, charts, and file management. The more important lesson was learning when to rely on AI and when to do the work myself.

For 1A, Donna's recorded role was limited to reviewing the assignment before submission, checking for missing requirements or irrelevant content, and checking spelling and grammar. It also handled formatting, PDF review, and repository uploads. I reviewed the presentation and requested proper essay formatting. That was useful because I could concentrate on reviewing the finished document instead of spending more time managing files.

In 1B and 1C, AI helped me research job listings and visualize my skills. The comparison tables, SWOT matrix, and skills chart made the gaps in my current abilities easier to see. However, presentation can create false confidence. A clean chart does not make my self-ratings objective, and a few job advertisements cannot describe an entire industry. I had to correct details, including C versus C++, and distinguish what employers actually requested from what I hoped to learn. I used the source listings and my own experience to review those conclusions.

The 1D conversation showed a different limitation. AI questioned whether reliability and understanding should come before optimization. I cared about reliability, but I disagreed with the idea that understanding must be finished before building. I learn by researching, trying things, making mistakes, fixing them, and rebuilding. AI's challenge was useful because I could push back and get a recommendation that better matched how I work. The discussion clarified my interest in automation, but it did not replace my judgment.

For 1E, verification became even more important. I separated the memory workflows that function in my setup from the unresolved problem of outdated retrieval results. I also kept my programming and debugging scores realistic. Repository evidence, contribution records, job postings, and research saved in Zotero were stronger support than a confident AI response. Studying weight-space programming is an ongoing research interest, not evidence that I have implemented it. I am currently building my own Rust retrieval project called Enigma-MCP, which is still in development.

The more I have used AI, the more I have returned to traditional learning, including hand coding and working through problems myself. That might sound backward, but it helps me understand what the tool is doing and recognize when an answer is weak. I still use AI to explore ideas and make better use of limited time. I just do not want speed to become a substitute for understanding. My goal is to become more capable at the work, not simply faster at producing documents.
