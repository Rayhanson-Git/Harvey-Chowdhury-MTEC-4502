## 1. Résumé comparative analysis

David Teather’s public software engineering résumé is my effective example [R1]. Its single-column format, clear section headings and aligned dates make the experience and projects easy to find. Action-led bullets connect technical decisions with results instead of presenting an unexplained tool list. The build-pipeline example names the work and gives a before-and-after duration; the MCP project identifies the service and its scope. Some bullets use dense technical language, but the one-page structure keeps the document concise and the contribution clear. I applied that lesson by giving Enigma and JimBro priority and making my employment more specific. My Rosedale entry now explains that I taught mathematics and physics and initiated the computer lab, rather than only saying I taught. Those details communicate an actual contribution without adding an invented outcome or metric.

Chirayu Babu Jaysawal’s earlier public résumé is my ineffective example for a focused early-career engineering application [R2]. It has clear headings and project links, but six projects, secondary-school marks, a broad skills list and generic soft skills compete for attention. Several descriptions repeat a project’s purpose without making the implementation or contribution clear. The crowded project section weakens readability, and broad claims about production quality make the work harder to assess. I would select fewer projects and replace general claims with specific responsibilities. My revised résumé follows that approach: three distinct technical projects, a compact contact line with readable links, and a consulting entry focused on U.S. and Canadian college applications and student-visa guidance. My portfolio supplies additional UI/UX and creative evidence without requiring every project on the résumé. This comparison judges the document’s clarity and relevance, not the author’s ability or their newer résumé.

## 4. Professional development priorities

This plan adapts the earlier Rust-prototype milestone because Enigma now has an inspected implementation. The next phase is bounded implementation, measurement and validation. Weeks below begin when I start this plan; they are planning windows, not new course deadlines. I will stay within the previously stated 3-4 project hours weekly and $20-$60 monthly total AI budget, checking those limits before expanding scope.

### 1. Programming independence - M2

**Action:** During weeks 1-2 of this plan, choose one bounded Enigma change, such as a retrieval filter or structured-record validation case. Write the expected input, output and failure behavior before changing it.

**Evidence of progress:** Keep the patch, focused checks and a short walkthrough explaining the data flow, error handling and design choice. Record where assistance was needed. Reassess level 3 only when I can implement and explain the module with checks.

**Career connection:** Applied AI engineering requires maintaining the implementation beneath an agent or retrieval workflow. A finished repository is useful evidence, but I need to explain and change its behavior.

### 2. Retrieval relevance and freshness - A4 / T4

**Action:** During weeks 2-4, prepare 20 labeled queries: five current facts, five historical facts, five changed or conflicting facts, and five irrelevant or unknown-answer cases. Define acceptable evidence and expected uncertainty before running retrieval.

**Evidence of progress:** Run keyword-only and hybrid retrieval on the same small dataset. Save queries, labels, settings, retrieved sources and error analysis. Use recall@k and MRR for retrieval, plus explicit checks for stale answers and unsupported certainty. Keep this separate from existing fixture and private-vault scores.

**Career connection:** My target work includes RAG and agent memory. I need to show why a result is useful and when the system should retain uncertainty. Enigma’s cache freshness and date filtering do not establish temporal truth resolution.

### 3. Repeatable evaluation and handoff - M5 / T3

**Action:** During weeks 4-6, turn one exploratory failure into a repeatable regression case. Run a focused suite before and after a bounded change, then ask another person to follow the documented setup or recovery steps.

**Evidence of progress:** Keep the command, environment, expected and actual result, failing/passing outputs, and a brief explanation of the correction. Record the other person’s task difficulties and one justified revision. No participant results are claimed yet.

**Career connection:** Reliable integrations require regression protection and a usable handoff. Passing CI supports the project; personally interpreting failures and observing setup will strengthen my evaluation evidence.
